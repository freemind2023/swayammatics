Traditional automation follows fixed rules: if X happens, do Y. It's reliable and predictable, but it breaks down when a task requires judgment or varies too much to script.

## Where AI agents fit in

AI agents handle tasks that involve interpreting unstructured input — an email, a document, a customer message — and deciding what to do next. Examples include:

- Reading an incoming support request and routing it to the right team with context attached
- Extracting structured data from invoices or contracts that don't follow a fixed template
- Answering internal questions by searching your own documentation

## They're not a replacement for process automation

Most real workflows combine both: a rule-based automation handles the predictable steps, and an AI agent handles the step that requires interpretation. Treating AI agents as a drop-in replacement for your whole workflow usually produces something harder to trust than either approach alone.

## How to decide which you need

If you can write the rule in a sentence ("if the invoice is under $500, approve it automatically"), you need automation. If you can't write the rule because the input varies too much, that's where an AI agent earns its place.
